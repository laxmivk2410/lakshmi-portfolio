Overview

What this project is about

YourGynac is a women's healthcare booking app on the Apple App Store. My job was to design the complete UX — from how a woman discovers the app to how she books an appointment with a gynaecologist and manages her health journey.

This was one of the most challenging projects I've worked on — not technically, but humanly. Healthcare UX for women requires a level of trust, clarity, and sensitivity that most other apps don't demand. Every design decision had to earn the user's confidence.

The core challenge: Women searching for gynaecology care are often dealing with something personal and sometimes stressful. The app needed to feel like a safe, professional space — not clinical, not casual, but exactly right.

The Challenge

What made this hard

🤝
Building trust instantly
Users open the app when they need care. There's no time to earn trust slowly. The first screen has to communicate safety and professionalism immediately.
🗣️
Getting the language right
Healthcare copy is notoriously hard. Too clinical and it's cold. Too casual and it undermines credibility. Every label, button text, and error message needed careful thought.
📋
Complex booking flow
Doctor discovery, specialisation filtering, appointment scheduling, and health history input — all in a single flow that felt simple and not overwhelming.
📱
iOS design standards
This was my first iOS-focused app. Meeting Apple's HIG guidelines while maintaining a unique visual identity required extra research and care.
My Process

How I approached it

1
Research — understanding women's healthcare needs
Studied existing healthcare apps (Practo, Apollo) to understand what was working and what felt cold or confusing. Identified that most healthcare apps felt transactional — missing the emotional reassurance users needed.
2
Define — mapping the booking journey
Created a user persona (25–40 year old woman, working professional, first-time or returning user). Mapped her full journey from "I need to see a doctor" to "appointment confirmed" — identifying every anxiety point along the way.
3
Colour and tone — designing for trust
Chose a calm, professional colour palette — deep teal and soft whites. Avoided the harsh clinical blues of typical healthcare apps. Typography was warm but readable. Every visual choice was intentional.
4
Screens — from wireframes to high-fidelity
Designed onboarding, doctor discovery, profile pages, booking flow, confirmation, and health history screens. Each screen went through at least 2 rounds of iteration before handoff.
5
Handoff and launch
Prepared a detailed Figma handoff — component specs, spacing documentation, interaction notes. The app launched on the Apple App Store and is live for download.
What I Learned

Honest takeaways

❤️
Design for the emotional state, not just the task
A user opening a gynaecology app isn't just "booking a doctor." She might be anxious, in pain, or confused. Designing for that emotional state changes everything.
🍎
iOS is opinionated, and that's a good thing
Apple's HIG forced me to think carefully about navigation patterns and component sizes. Following platform conventions actually made the app feel more trustworthy to users.
✍️
UX writing is UX design
The words on every screen matter as much as the layout. "Select a doctor" vs "Find your doctor" — the same instruction, completely different feeling. I became much more deliberate about language.
📦
Shipping to App Store is a different challenge
Apple's review process has strict requirements. Designing to those standards from the beginning saved significant back-and-forth during the submission process.
