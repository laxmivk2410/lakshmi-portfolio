Overview

What this project is about

Queen Ludo is a mobile Ludo game designed for casual play. My job was to design the complete UI — everything a player sees from the moment they open the app to the moment they win or lose.

This was one of my end-to-end game UI projects. I owned the entire visual experience — lobby, matchmaking, gameplay board, in-game HUD, power-ups, win/lose screens, and onboarding.

Game UI is one of the most demanding forms of mobile design. Everything needs to feel immediately understandable — users don't read instructions, they just start tapping. The first interaction needs to be understandable quickly, with clear hierarchy and feedback.

Challenges

What made this hard

📱

Small screen, lots of information. A Ludo board has 4 players, dice, tokens, and a path system — all on a phone screen. Getting everything readable without feeling cramped took multiple iterations.

⚡

Fast feedback loops. Users needed to immediately understand what happened after every action — dice roll, token move, token capture. Each moment needed a visual response.

🎨

Standing out in a crowded category. There are hundreds of Ludo games on the Play Store. The visual design needed to feel premium and distinct — not like a generic reskin.

⏰

Tight timeline. This was a fast-paced production environment. I had to design, iterate, and hand off within weeks — no months of exploration.

Design Decisions

Key choices and why I made them

Rich, dark colour palette

Instead of the typical bright primary colours common in Ludo games, I chose a deeper, richer palette with jewel tones. It made the game feel more premium and less like a children's toy — targeting young adult players.

Clear token state indicators

Every token has three states: at home, on the path, and safe. Each state gets a distinct visual treatment so players never have to wonder if their token is at risk. Clear states = less confusion = more engagement.

Celebration moments on wins

The win screen was designed to feel genuinely satisfying — not just a "you won" text. Confetti, trophy animation, and a clear score breakdown. Small moments of delight keep players coming back.

One-thumb friendly layout

Most mobile gaming happens one-handed. Every interactive element — dice roll button, token tap targets — was sized and positioned for comfortable single-thumb use, even on smaller phones.

Result

What shipped

Queen Ludo launched on Google Play Store and is live for download. It was one of the first games I shipped solo — from initial concepts to final developer handoff.

What I Learned

Honest takeaways

🎮
Game design is about emotion

More than any other UI I've designed, game UI is about how the player feels in every moment. Winning should feel great. Losing should feel survivable. Every design decision serves that emotional arc.

🔄
Iterate fast, decide faster

Game production is fast. I learned to make decisions quickly, move on, and revisit only what really needed it. Perfectionism is expensive when you have a deadline.

👁️
Visual hierarchy is everything

In a game with lots happening on screen at once, visual hierarchy is what keeps things readable. I got much better at controlling where the eye goes — and when.

🤝
Good developer handoff saves everyone

Precise specs, named components, and documented interactions meant developers could build without constantly asking questions. The handoff quality directly affects the final product quality.
